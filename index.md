## Research and methods

My research examines how political institutions shape and are shaped by national identity, migration, and political behavior. My regional focus is East Asia, especially the Korean Peninsula, while my comparative work extends to other jurisdictions. See my [current projects](/projects/) for work on immigration attitudes, nationalism and political change, and Korean history textbooks.

I build research corpora from archival and other hard-to-use sources, combining OCR, vision-language models, and large language models to analyze texts that were previously difficult to study. I also conduct applied policy research on innovation policy, firm growth, and labour markets. My [publications and open research resources](/research/) provide access to papers, data, and tools.

## Teaching and public engagement

At Leiden, I teach computational text analysis, digital humanities, research design, and international relations. I supervise BA, MA, and PhD research. My [teaching page](/teaching/) has course resources and [information for prospective supervisees](/teaching/#supervision).

I develop open educational resources, Korean-studies corpora, and tools for experimental design and manuscript review. I also write [*Pixels and Patterns*](https://www.pixelsandpatterns.org/), a Substack on using AI responsibly in research and computational work.

## Background and affiliations

I earned a Ph.D. in Political Science from the University of Toronto, an M.A. in Global Affairs and Policy from Yonsei University, and a B.A. in Political Science from Harding University.

Beyond Leiden, I serve as Director of Research at [SinoNK.com](https://sinonk.com), a Senior Fellow at the [European Centre for North Korean Studies](https://ecnk.univie.ac.at/) at the University of Vienna, and a Non-Resident Fellow in the [Innovation Policy Lab](https://munkschool.utoronto.ca/ipl/) at the University of Toronto.
