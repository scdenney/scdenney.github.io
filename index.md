I am an assistant professor at [Leiden University](https://www.universiteitleiden.nl/) and an affiliated researcher with the [Centre for Digital Humanities (LUCDH)](https://www.universiteitleiden.nl/en/humanities/centre-for-digital-humanities). I earned a Ph.D. in Political Science from the University of Toronto, an M.A. in Global Affairs and Policy from Yonsei University, and a B.A. in Political Science from Harding University.

I study how political institutions shape national identity and belonging — who counts as a member of the nation (i.e., the "circle of we"), and on what terms. Most of my work asks how political change, especially democratization, reshapes national identity, attitudes toward immigration, and political behavior. I focus on East Asia, with an emphasis on the Korean Peninsula, but the questions regularly take me other cases, such as Germany and the United States.

A second thread of my work is methodological and focuses on developing new ways to measure identity and belonging, primarily through conjoint and other survey experiments, and increasingly through computational text analysis. This work sits at the intersection of comparative politics and digital humanities. Recently it has meant building research corpora from archival and other hard-to-use sources, combining OCR, vision-language models, and large language models to work with texts that were previously difficult to analyze at scale.

Alongside my primary research, I maintain a portfolio of applied policy research on innovation, entrepreneurship, and — most recently — the impact of AI on the labour market and the digital economy.

My teaching runs downstream of my research. At Leiden, I teach computational text analysis and digital humanities, including an introduction to text-as-data using Korean-language sources, as well as courses in research design and international relations. I supervise BA and MA theses across four programs, in International Studies, Korean Studies, Asian Studies, and International Relations. I advise Ph.D candidates whose work aligns with my own on a rolling-basis. I also develop and maintain open educational resources for research methods and computational text analysis, including curated Korean-studies corpora and open-source tools for experimental design and pre-submission manuscript review.

Beyond Leiden, I serve as Director of Research at [SinoNK.com](http://sinonk.com), a Senior Fellow at the [European Centre for North Korean Studies](https://ecnk.univie.ac.at/) at the University of Vienna, and a Non-Resident Fellow in the [Innovation Policy Lab](https://munkschool.utoronto.ca/ipl/) at the University of Toronto. These affiliations reflect my commitment to publicly engaged scholarship and policy-relevant research.
